\begin{abstract}
Tool-augmented language models are commonly organized as a sequence of discrete rounds: the model reasons, serializes a tool call, waits for a result, appends the observation, and resumes generation. This interface is poorly matched to diffusion language models, whose intermediate states can evolve non-causally and remain revisable while external computation is in flight. We introduce \emph{Continuous Interaction Diffusion}, abbreviated CID, as a model--runtime co-designed architecture for continuous and asynchronous tool-augmented reasoning. CID separates model state into three coupled channels: an externally controlled, model-read-only fact channel; a continuous thought channel represented by a \emph{Typed Cognitive Tensor}; and a discrete display channel that converges to the user-visible response. Information needs emerge as typed latent states rather than fully serialized calls. A runtime converts persistent needs into \emph{perceptual bindings}, refreshes static or changing sources according to binding policies, and repeatedly projects their results into the evolving thought state. Consequently, tool use becomes sustained perception rather than a sequence of blocking remote procedure calls. We formalize the channel semantics, binding lifecycle, asynchronous state transitions, and training objectives, and provide a falsifiable evaluation protocol. This first paper focuses on read-only tools and defines the architecture without making empirical performance claims.
\end{abstract}
