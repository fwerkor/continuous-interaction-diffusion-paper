\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Read-Only Evaluation Scope.}
Our experiments deliberately restrict external execution to read-only retrieval, files, databases, calculators, clocks, environment state, and streams. This is an evaluation boundary rather than an architectural restriction of CID: the current study isolates sustained asynchronous perception and revisable generation from the additional semantics of irreversible external actions. In a side-effecting setting, a tool request can remain a revisable typed action intent inside the TCT while denoising continues, with the runtime retaining authority over the transition from a tentative intent to an externally committed action. Such a commit boundary can incorporate authorization, argument validation, user confirmation, idempotency keys, and recovery policy before execution. Persistent state is particularly useful here because repeatedly maintaining or refining an action intent need not imply repeated execution of the corresponding external effect. Once committed, the resulting state transition or acknowledgement can re-enter CID as an external event and trigger the same dependency-aware revision mechanisms used for observations. We do not evaluate this extension here; establishing safe and reliable commit semantics for side-effecting tools remains an important direction for future work~\citep{yao2024taubench,lu2024toolsandbox}.

\paragraph{Human Readability of Latent Reasoning.}
CID's thought channel is a continuous typed latent state rather than a natural-language chain of thought. This enables local revision, parallel evolution, explicit uncertainty, and persistent source bindings, but it removes a property that conventional textual reasoning traces provide by construction: direct human readability. A user or developer cannot inspect the TCT and interpret its full reasoning process in the same way as a sequence of natural-language intermediate steps. Typed roles, symbolic anchors, source links, action intents, uncertainty estimates, and dependency metadata can expose structured summaries of what the model is attending to or attempting to do, but these observables are not equivalent to a faithful textual rationale. Improving the interpretability of continuous reasoning states, for example through dedicated probes or distilled human-readable traces, is therefore a separate research problem and is not addressed by the present evaluation.
